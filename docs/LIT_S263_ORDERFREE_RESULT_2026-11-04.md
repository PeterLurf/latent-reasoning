# §263 ORDER-FREE result vs the premise-order / binding literature

Literature, written 2026-11-04 00:31 EST, at Theory's request. The result being compared is Note 263-3 (292b2d7d). The four papers were checked against their arXiv abstracts on 2026-11-04 (abstract level, not full text). Our own bibliography was grepped first: these IDs appear in LITERATURE.md (§263 citations) and EXPERIMENT_BACKLOG.md lines 683 and 697.

**One line:** §263 confirms Premise Order Matters (the pre-§263 lineage had the same dependence, in an extreme form) and confirms He et al. within the augmented scope. It qualifies He et al. on two points: permutation augmentation alone failed in our lineage (§252 NP, §254) and needed the one-hop lookup co-training, and the gain did not transfer to unaugmented orderings (full shuffle). It parallels Wu/Geiger/Millière phase 2 → 3, behaviourally only. It does not test Dai et al.'s Ordering-ID claim; that is a cheap $0 lead. Nothing in it contradicts any of the four papers.

**Wording advice for the C1 scope line:** "order-free over op sentences, learned with permutation augmentation plus one-hop name-lookup co-training (cf. order-centric augmentation, 2502.19907); orderings outside the augmented scope (full shuffle) remain order-bound, as Premise Order Matters (2402.08939) reports for LLMs". Make no "first" claim about order invariance. The training remedy itself is prior art (2502.19907); what is new is its scope, a looped latent model with a per-pass name cue.

**For the planned perm_scope-all follow-up:** He et al. permute independent premises only (DAG-valid). Permuting all sentences (givens and S_0 after the op that uses them) is a stronger augmentation than theirs. Their results don't predict whether it works here, so state it as an open bet.
