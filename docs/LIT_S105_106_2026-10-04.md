# Prior checks for §105 (GEN-4-MASK on S5, C1) and §106 (bounded-state streaming multiplication, C2) (2026-10-04)

Literature session, for Theory; Audit copied. Brief. IDs verified (arXiv abstract pages; earlier notes).

## §105: latent-only (masked) decoders as a bypass control
- **Priors:**
  - SIM-CoT 2509.20317: an auxiliary decoder conditioned on the latent, dropped at inference.
  - Probes on the latent alone (linear readouts; Hewitt & Liang 1909.03368 controls).
  - **The input-only control:** 2509.13316 shows verbalisers can be right *without* target internals, from the input
    alone. That is the reason a decoder must be denied the visible inputs.
  - Interventions: Li 2602.08783 (step do-interventions on Coconut/CODI); SCIT 2608.27265 (CODI's computation sits in
    the value cache, not the latents, i.e. a bypass located by interchange).
  - No latent-CoT paper uses a masked fork as its main bypass control. New as a control design.
- **Risk: yes, MASK can move recomputation into the slot's own forward pass.**
  - With the chain unchanged, slot j is computed at op j's last token with full attention to the visible ops and to
    earlier slots. It can recompute the state in-pass instead of receiving it from slot j−1.
  - So a MASK pass shows "the state is *decodable from the slot alone*", not "it was *carried by the hand-off*".
  - The hand-off claim needs §104's donor protocol on slot j−1 → slot j: an interchange at j−1 that changes slot j's
    decode to the donor-consistent state.
  - In-pass recomputation over 4 S5 ops is unlikely for a pretrained transformer (2602.14814: they fail S5 tracking
    in text). But HYB-4 s5's mean-ablation result (1.000) shows a bypass exists *through the fork*, so test it rather
    than assume.
  - **Pass wording:** "latent-only decodable (MASK) + donor-consistent interchange at j−1 → hand-off".

## §106: bounded-state streaming multiplication
- **Length-generalisation priors for multiplication:**
  - **Jelassi et al. 2306.15400.** Train 5 × 3 → test **35 × 3** (the *first* operand grows, the second fixed at 3
    digits), but only with **train-set priming** (10–50 long examples). Relative PE alone fails for multiplication.
    The closest "one operand grows, the other fixed" result. Yours grows b with a fixed at 4.
  - Cho 2410.15787: position coupling + scratchpad, 10 × 10 → 20 × 15.
  - Lee 2502.01612: self-improvement, 5 × 5 → 9–10 × 9–10.
  - Turing Programs 2407.03310: a Turing-machine-style scratchpad (copy with small edits) length-generalises
    addition and multiplication.
  - **Neural GPU 1511.08228:** binary multiplication trained ≤ 20 bits, error-free on much longer numbers. A
    convolutional recurrent model with a bounded local state: the architectural ancestor of "bounded carry,
    streamed".
- **Does a written-carry CoT already extrapolate with a fixed-depth transformer?**
  - With a history cut, yes in principle. It is our written-state loop (§66/§71: exact far beyond training when every
    step is local and the state bounded).
  - Without a cut, positional range limits it (Jelassi needs priming; Cho needs position coupling).
  - **So the comparator must include text CoT in the same streaming format *with* a history cut** (WC-format), not
    only full-history text CoT. Otherwise C2 is credited to the latents when the format plus cut does the work.
- **"Hand-built state?"** Scoping it as a restartable CoT data format, a design choice like a dataset's own CoT
  (Turing Programs, Cho, Lee also choose formats), is fair. C2 then belongs to the format plus cut, and the latents'
  contribution is C1/C3, shown against WC-format at matched accuracy.
- **Pitfalls:**
  - (a) The emitted answer digits grow with |b|. If they're appended in-context, answer positions leave the trained
    range, so assemble the answer outside the chunk.
  - (b) The final flush (remaining carry digits once b ends) is a different step type. Make sure it's in training.
  - (c) Carry ≤ len(a) + 1 digits holds for a = 4. Test also a = 5 at b = 8 for the distribution shift in carry width.
