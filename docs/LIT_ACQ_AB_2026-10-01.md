# Prior check: §70 proposals A (operator-conditioned exact likelihood) and B (tempered posterior → hard EM) (2026-10-01)

## A. Per-operation transition matrices vs additive vs multiplicative cells
- **Direct precedent for the comparison:** Goudreau, Giles, Chakradhar & Chen 1994, *First-order versus second-order
  single-layer recurrent neural networks* (IEEE TNN, non-arXiv). Single-layer *first-order* (additive) RNNs cannot
  represent all DFAs; *second-order* (state × input) RNNs can. Also Miller & Giles 1993 (an experimental comparison of
  order) and Giles et al. 1992 (*Neural Computation*; second-order RNN DFA induction and extraction).
- **Per-input transition matrices:** **Input Switched Affine Networks**, Foerster et al. 1611.09434 (one affine map per
  input symbol, i.e. your B_a). Multiplicative RNNs (Sutskever, Martens & Hinton, ICML 2011, non-arXiv); multiplicative
  integration, Wu et al. 1606.06630.
- **State-tracking evidence that input-dependent transitions are what matters:** input-dependent S4 (IDS4) solves
  A5/A4×Z5 with one layer where S4 and Mamba cannot (Merrill 2404.08819); DeltaProduct 2502.10297 (input-dependent
  Householder products for S3–S5); Grazzi 2411.12537.
- **Certificate:** no study found that compares additive, multiplicative and per-input parameterisations *under
  exact-likelihood training from terminal labels with a full automaton certificate*. New as scoped. The 1990s comparisons
  used extraction or accuracy, not exhaustive certificates.
- **Prediction from the priors:** the per-input (second-order / ISAN-like) cell should certify where the additive cell
  fails, consistent with your bilinear result. That supports "parameterisation, not exploration" as directional.

## B. Tempered posterior (η > 1 sharpening) continuing into hard EM
- **The same family:**
  - **Unified EM**, Samdani, Chang & Roth 2012 (NAACL, non-arXiv): a single parameter spans soft EM ↔ hard EM (and
    constrained EM). Your q_η ∝ w^η is that spectrum.
  - **Deterministic annealing**, Rose 1998 (Proc. IEEE; non-arXiv): temperature → 0 continues soft assignments into
    hard ones; Ueda & Nakano 1998.
  - **Lateen EM**, Spitkovsky, Alshawi & Jurafsky 2011 (EMNLP, non-arXiv): alternates soft and hard EM objectives "when
    stuck" to escape local optima in grammar induction. The closest precedent for switching into Viterbi to escape.
  - Viterbi training, Spitkovsky 2010; Smith & Eisner 2004 (annealing).
- **Known failure mode:** hard EM and high-η freezing. Like k-means, it locks a wrong partition once assignments are
  hard. Annealing toward hard *after* a good soft solution is the safe direction. Lateen EM's evidence is that switching
  objectives helps when the current one is stuck, not that early hardening helps. Keep your η = 1 GEM control and the
  marginal-likelihood anchor; report when the partition freezes (the path entropy collapse step).
- **New if scoped as:** an η > 1 sharpening schedule into Viterbi for *certified permutation-automaton induction*. The
  method family is established.

## Exact expected reward J vs log-likelihood log J
- **Guu et al. 2017** 1704.07926: the MML gradient weights each correct latent by its normalised posterior p(z|x,
  correct); RL weights it by p(z|x)·R. RandoMer's β interpolates between the two.
- **Misra et al. 2018**, *Policy Shaping and Generalized Update Equations for Semantic Parsing from Denotations*,
  1809.01299 (EMNLP): a generalised update equation unifying MML, RL and hybrid weightings. **The standard citation**
  for J vs log J.
- Also RAML, Norouzi et al. 1609.00150 (reward-augmented ML vs expected reward).

## Addendum (2026-10-01, after §70 registration 0f4dae7): a prior for A that I missed
- **Howe 2609.18966** (preprint, single author, 2026-07). A 1-layer Householder-product linear RNN with and without
  the additive input term b_t = W_b e_t, on parity, S4, A5 and S5 generator words, trained at length 32 and tested at
  512 over 5 seeds.
  - **With b_t:** the model fits and then collapses (S5 0.20 at 512).
  - **Without it** (input acts only through the transitions): the exact automaton at 16×, S5 1.00 (5/5).
  - Starting from a verified-exact solution, Adam *grows* the additive path and destroys generalisation; zeroing W_b
    at inference restores it in 18/20 seeds.
- **Bearing on A:** this is the same additive vs input-through-transition contrast, already shown under SGD with
  length generalisation. It predicts your additive cell fails by shortcut, not by exploration. A's remaining novelty is
  **exact likelihood from terminal labels plus a full automaton certificate** (Howe uses per-position labels and
  accuracy at 512, not a certificate). Cite Howe in §70's prior block.
