# Prior check for §125: standardising the carried FSQ embedding to GPT-2's token statistics (soft-phase no-read basin) (2026-10-05)

Literature session, for Theory; Audit copied. Brief. IDs verified (abstract pages / LIT_INDEX). Diagnosis: the carried
embedding has a norm of about 29 against about 4 for tokens, with the input-dependent part about 3.9. A large,
nearly constant vector buries the signal.

## (1) Priors: input-scale or distribution mismatch makes injected vectors ignored or unstable
- **Soft prompts:** Lester 2104.08691 (prompt tuning is sensitive to initialisation; vocab-sampled init beats random).
  Prefix-tuning 2101.00190 (directly optimised prefixes are unstable, so they are reparameterised through an MLP).
- **Latent-CoT injection:**
  - HRPO 2505.18454: raw hidden-state injection "yields nonsensical rollouts"; a *normalised* vocab projection trains.
  - CODI 2502.21074: an MLP + LayerNorm projection on the fed-back latents.
  - LT-Tuning 2602.10229: distribution mismatch from reusing hidden states causes "feature collapse".
  - Coconut feeds raw states.
- **Anisotropy and massive activations:** Ethayarajh 1909.00512, Timkey 2109.04404, Sun 2402.17762. Our §72b/§73
  (standardisation 0/5 → 10/10; centring alone suffices) and §89 (g(h) rescaled to token-embedding std).
- **Normalising inputs before a readout:** MAE 2111.06377 (affine-free BN before the probe).
- **The decoder ignoring a weak latent:** posterior collapse (Bowman 1511.06349); lagging inference (He 1901.05534).
- **Verdict:** well supported. Standardising injected vectors to the embedding distribution is standard practice. Its
  use to escape a soft-phase no-read basin in an FSQ carrier is new as scoped.

## (2) RECON: worth an arm, with a caveat
- Reconstructing the standardised h (768 dims) through an 8-dim z_b forces the bottleneck to keep h's **top principal
  components**.
  - These may be dominated by **nuisance variance** (position, format, op identity) rather than the state, spending
    the code's capacity on the wrong things.
  - It doesn't defeat FSQ's discreteness (rounding still applies), but it can slow canonical codes (more codes per
    state).
- **Better, still task-agnostic: predict the *next* segment's hidden state** (Next-Latent Prediction 2511.05963; LIFT
  2609.38149). It is state-relevant by construction, because the next step depends on the state. Or use RECON with a
  small weight, **decayed to 0 once hard rounding starts**.
- If you keep RECON, log codes per state against the plain STD arm.

## (3) EMA statistics across the soft → hard transition
- Rounding changes z_b's distribution at about 4k, so the EMA statistics lag and the network briefly sees mismatched
  inputs. Test-time statistics frozen too early would carry soft-phase values.
- This is the classic BN train/eval and small-batch mismatch (Batch Renormalization 1702.03275).
- **Fixes:**
  - (i) Reset or re-estimate the EMA at the transition (a faster momentum for a few hundred steps).
  - (ii) Freeze the test statistics from the **last N hard-phase batches** only.
  - (iii) Alternatively, use a **statistics-free** version, e′ = μ_E + sd_E ⊙ LayerNorm(e). It is per-sample, with no
    train/test mismatch. It removes the scale problem, but not per-coordinate offsets (§73 found centring
    load-bearing), so compare it as a variant.
