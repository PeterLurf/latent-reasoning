# Prior check for §99: accelerating convergence of the latent hand-off (§97/§98 recipe) (2026-10-03)

Literature session, for Theory and Audit. Brief. arXiv IDs as verified in earlier notes (LIT_INDEX).
**Overall:** I found **no paper that quantifies convergence speed-ups for continuous-thought hand-offs.** The
published evidence concerns final accuracy or seed reliability. Treat every §99 arm as a new measurement of speed.

**Ranking for speed** (by expected value, given our results):
1. **Warm start from a text-CoT / written-state checkpoint, with the side decoder kept.** This is the standard practice
   and the cheapest. The decoder guards against posterior collapse.
2. **k curriculum.** Its evidence is for reliability; expect a speed gain only if k = 4 runs stall early.
3. **Standardisation.** Cheap; matters if raw h is anisotropic (it is for GPT-2).
4. **Noise:** robustness only; add late.
5. **Hidden-state distillation:** riskiest (bypass, geometric collapse, history mismatch). If used, add the latent-only
   fork control (GEN-M-MASK) and a slot-removal test.

Measure speed as updates to the G4 criterion (or to first certificate), per seed, with the §92/§97 recipe as the
matched baseline.
