## Five things to act on

1. **2609.33144 (*Beyond the Training Horizon*, Sept 2026) is the closest competitor to both streams. Read it in full.**
   It is a mechanistic study of looped transformers failing past their training depth, and includes knowledge-graph
   traversal. Its decoupled-recurrence model runs a "propagating state frontier" whose intermediate confidence decays
   along the chain (peak correct-entity probability 0.98 at hop 5 → 0.71 at hop 16), and a "fresh–aged" residual
   direction restores a stale state's usefulness. These are near-analogues of the experiments stream's propagation limit
   and the theory stream's Mechanism 2 (fade, restoration). What they do not have: seeds (no seed variability
   reported), Coconut-style thoughts with access to earlier thoughts, a retrieval schedule, interventions that are null
   in-distribution, or any fix. Both streams must cite it and state these differences.
2. **The "unity contract" (PROPOSALS T2, Pilot A arm MB) is not new.** Matched recurrence (T = n) is the training
   scheme of Fan et al. 2409.15647, of Zhang et al. 2607.20594 ("exactly unity under T = n") and of MR-Loop in 2609.33144.
   What is ours is the *reason* it helps (slack → step-indexed schedules) and the causal evidence for that reason.
3. **Anytime supervision (T5) has close relatives that must be cited.** RELAY 2502.08482 (iteration-wise supervision
   gives looped transformers length generalisation beyond the training length) is the most direct. Others: PonderNet
   2107.05407 (a loss at every step), TRM-style deep supervision, CLRS hints, and Bansal et al.'s progressive loss.
   The defensible novelty is the minimum-progress form (masked look-ahead, early arrival never penalised) plus its
   mechanistic motivation. Pilot E (PE1) is the test that makes it a result.
4. **P7 reads the far chain, so it is an out-of-distribution probe, not an in-distribution instrument.** State this
   plainly. Your own T4 did the head-to-head the literature needs: "completion time at depth 6", effectively Zhang et
   al.'s convergence-time instrument ported to this setting, reaches AUC 0.82 against P7's 0.99. That is a citable
   finding: in-distribution head instruments predict less well here than in Zhang et al. (AUROC up to 1.00). Frame it
   as a comparison with them, not as a first.
5. **Prop. 1 (slack ⇒ continuation not identified) is an instance of underspecification** (D'Amour et al. 2011.03395).
   Cite it as the general principle. The contribution is naming the specific family (step-indexed schedules), where it
   comes from (curriculum stage 3) and a causal rescue.

## Addendum (2026-09-29, afternoon) — reply to the theory session's follow-up

**Prop. 4 (random budgets help iff their support includes the tight budget at circuit selection) checked against the
papers' actual schedules.** Training details are from the full HTML of both papers, read through a summariser.
- **Kuo et al. 2606.29983:** stochastic K = clip(n + Δ, 1, 20), Δ ~ Unif{−5, …, 5}; train n < 20, test n = 20–60; no
  length curriculum. The support includes K < n and K = n, so it is tight or undershooting if the loops needed are ≈ n
  (the paper does not state the minimum; their learned halting puts more loops on longer inputs). **Complication:** their
  length-matched schedule K = n is the *worst* OOD (22.1%, highest variance), below fixed K = 20 (34.2%, maximal slack)
  and below stochastic stopping and RL-halting (45.0%). A deterministic tight budget tied to an observable (length) makes
  the loop count encode length, which is the spurious length–loop correlation they blame. So "tight" alone is not
  sufficient. Suggested refinement: random budgets help iff (a) the support reaches the tight budget (or below it) at
  selection **and** (b) the budget is not a deterministic function of an observable the model can count. Our MB arm
  (K = stage depth + 1, constant within a stage while example depths vary) satisfies (b); a per-example K = depth + 1
  would not.
- **DR-Loop (2609.33144):** R ~ clip(Poisson(λ = 4), 2, 8), independent of length (n ≤ 12), hop-depth curriculum
  gated at 0.95. The frontier advances ~1–2 positions per loop, so R = 2 undershoots most examples: the support includes
  tight and undershooting budgets. Consistent with Prop. 4. MR-Loop is T = n exactly (tight, deterministic in length),
  with a length curriculum from 3 to 12. Both extrapolate and degrade. There is no slack control, so this is not a test
  of Prop. 4.
- **Huginn 2502.05171 (extra case):** r ~ Poisson(e^τ) + 1 with τ log-normal, "most often … less than r̄" with a heavy
  tail. Mostly undershooting, plus random initial states (below). Consistent.
- **Latent randk (P22, K 4..8) and OV (6..8):** never tight at stage 3. Consistent. Prop. 4 fits all four schedules
  once condition (b) is added.

**Also relevant to Mechanism 1 / A1:** Kavuncu 2609.19521 (Sept 2026, cellular automata, trained to depth 6, tested to 12):
the recurrent transformer that carries only its current state (BUT) extrapolates (98.1% at depth 12); CoTFormer, with an
expanding cache of earlier recurrent states, falls to 50.3%. Correcting CoTFormer's state helps only until the retained
history undermines it, and clearing the cache restores extrapolation. This is independent evidence that a history channel
over earlier recurrent states harms extrapolation, which you can cite with the retrieval block. Note that there the
history was not used as an addressable memory: it interfered rather than being retrieved from.
