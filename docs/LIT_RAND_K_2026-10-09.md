# Prior check for RAND-K: randomising the think-pass count per batch (Huginn-style) for C2 beyond the trained K (2026-10-09)

Literature session, for Audit (the lead) and Theory.
- **IDs:** verified via arXiv abstract pages.
- **Huginn details:** from its HTML (Sec. 3–4, App.) (2026-10-09).

## 1. Huginn 2502.05171: exact sampling and truncation
- **Recurrence count per training step:** a **log-normal Poisson**. Given a target mean r̄ + 1 and **σ = ½**:
  - τ ~ 𝒩(log r̄ − ½σ², σ);
  - r ~ Poisson(e^τ) + 1.
  - It "most often samples values less than r̄", with a **heavy tail** of occasional much longer unrolls.
- **The main model:** layers (prelude, recurrent, coda) = (2, 4, 2), **r̄ = 32**; 132 effective layers at the mean (out-projection
  initialisation scaled by that).
- **Truncated backprop: only the last k iterations, with k = 8** ("we fix k = 8 in our main experiments"). Memory and backward compute are
  then independent of r. The prelude still gets gradients via the injected e at every step.
- **The state init:** s₀ ~ a truncated normal (σ_s² = 2/5), not zeros. Randomised initial states are part of the recipe (cf. §166's state
  coverage).
- Caveat: iso-depth 2604.21106 finds truncated backprop lowers the recurrence-equivalence exponent φ (0.46 → 0.38), so loops are trained
  less well. At our K ≤ 16, full backprop is affordable. Randomise K without truncation.

## 2. Fixed vs randomised (or progressive) recurrence: ablations on algorithmic tasks
- **Kuo et al. 2606.29983, "Stabilizing Extrapolation in Looped Transformers via Learned Stochastic Stopping": the direct ablation.**
  - Looped transformers length-generalise, but **OOD variance is high, even across equally good in-distribution solutions**. They trace it to
    the **spurious correlation between sequence length and loop count** in training.
  - **Stochastic loop counts in training sharply reduce OOD variance and stabilise predictions across inference-time loop counts.**
  - RL-halting (a learned stochastic schedule) improves the accuracy / stability trade-off further, but can stabilise a suboptimal computation.
  - Tasks: binary addition, Dyck-1, Unique Set, Copy.
- **Bansal et al. 2202.05826, "Logical Extrapolation Without Overthinking":**
  - fixed-iteration recurrent nets **degenerate when iterated many more times ("overthinking")**;
  - fixes: (i) **recall** (keep an explicit copy of the input, i.e. input injection) and (ii) **progressive training** (random iteration counts,
    with the loss on continuing from an intermediate state). This "prevents learning behaviours specific to the iteration number";
  - extrapolation on prefix sums, mazes and chess.
- **Schwarzschild et al. 2106.04537:** recurrent nets trained with few steps on easy problems solve harder ones by "thinking longer" (the
  original easy-to-hard result).
- **McLeish 2511.07384:** a curriculum of recurrences when retrofitting pretrained LMs. Fan 2409.15647: an adaptive, length-tied loop count.
- **Summary:**
  - Randomising (or progressively training) the iteration count is the established fix for iteration-specific behaviour.
  - Kuo is the cleanest fixed-vs-random ablation (on stability across loop counts).
  - No ablation on a *pretrained* LM latent reasoner was found. New for us.

## 3. Latent reasoners degrading past their trained iteration count, and the fixes used
- **PCCoT 2506.18582:** accuracy peaks near T = 3 Jacobi iterations and **fluctuates or unstable beyond T = 6** (their ablation; our §172).
- **Coconut / CODI:** our GSM8K_REPRO Amendment 3 / §13: accuracy peaks at the trained thought budget, with no gain past it.
- **Ouro 2510.25741:** degrades beyond the trained depth. **The recurrence retrofit 2608.11233:** about 1.5× the supervised depth, then decay.
- **The dynamics behind it:**
  - 2607.09698: Coconut is an *expanding* system, CODI an attractor;
  - "Right direction, wrong step" 2609.16665: finite-step overshoot; a fixed ¼ step recovers progress;
  - Think-at-Hard 2511.08577: "latent overthinking" (correct after pass 1, revised into errors later).
- **The fixes, in the literature:**
  - randomised or heavy-tailed K (Huginn; Kuo);
  - progressive training + recall (Bansal);
  - damping (2609.16665);
  - convergence or learned stochastic halting (AdaAnchor 2603.15051; Kuo's RL-halting; RecurTrace with oracle supervision; **not plain
    ACT / PonderNet**);
  - input injection (Bansal's recall; Fan).

## Design notes for RAND-K
1. **Use Huginn's sampler scaled down:** r̄ = the current K (say 8), σ = ½, plus a hard cap (e.g. 4r̄) for memory. Keep full backprop at our sizes.
2. **Decorrelate K from problem size (Kuo):**
   - if the training mix ties longer problems to more passes, randomise K *independently* of length;
   - otherwise the model learns "loops ∝ length" and OOD lengths read the schedule wrong.
3. **Progressive loss (Bansal) as a second arm:** run k₁ passes without grad from the start, then k₂ passes with grad. It trains
   "continue from any state", which is also what §166's state coverage targets.
4. **Report accuracy vs test K** (1 … 4K) per arm. The success criterion is a flat-or-rising curve past the trained mean (no overthinking), plus
   lower variance across seeds (Kuo's metric).
