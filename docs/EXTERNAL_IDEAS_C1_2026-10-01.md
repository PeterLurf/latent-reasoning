## Audit triage

* **Adopt now (C1 test definition).** A zero-thought ablation is not enough: our own finding 10 shows 1 thought reaching
  0.93–0.94 while 0 thoughts is at chance. C1 tests must add early-budget controls (T = 1, 2) and content interventions
  (donor-state swaps, irrelevant swaps), and separate "a one-pass baseline fails" from "information cannot arrive before
  round d".
* **Design 1 (priority).** A six-state categorical latent on S3, trained by exact forward marginalisation over a learned
  GPT-2 transition table (no straight-through), run hard at test. A full 36-transition audit gives an
  arbitrary-length certificate. It attacks both of our failure modes: credit assignment (§54, F1-S two attractors) and
  drift (§64). Caveats:
  - necessity comes from the streaming interface (one operation per call), so the stronger claim needs a full-prompt
    GPT-2 baseline to fail;
  - a tiny table/MLP control is required.
  - Natural follow-up: A5 (60 states), where our toys deadlocked.
* **Design 2.** Local categorical registers on Boolean trees (exact marginalisation on disjoint-child trees). The
  communication bound gives a necessity certificate, but the wiring is a heavy scaffold. After Design 1.
* **Design 3.** Continuous registers with self-trained repair (the same GPT-2 in repair mode). This is the direct test of
  the drift diagnosis without discretisation. Higher risk.
* **On our quantised-latent candidate.**
  - Snap after every transition first.
  - Use a union-bound target (ε ≤ 7.8e-5 per update for 99% at 128).
  - Excess codebook capacity can re-encode a counter.
  - The VQ and FSQ straight-through recipes risk repeating our failures.
  - This is consistent with Theory's §67 framing.

**I would run three experiments: an exactly marginalized six-state latent machine, a network of local categorical registers, and a continuous version with learned repair.** The first two can yield a strong correctness certificate after training; the third tests your drift diagnosis without assuming that discretization is essential. None is a known solution to your complete target. Their defensible necessity guarantees come from explicit streaming or locality restrictions. Whether they outperform an ordinary GPT-2 given the entire problem remains an empirical question. All use one pretrained GPT-2 small as the recurrent learned processor, with small embedding/output heads, and no intermediate oracle labels or textual reasoning states.

Your most useful next target is **a learned transition rule whose correctness can be audited completely**, rather than another aggregate extrapolation score. This would separate failure to learn the rule from failure to preserve it through repeated execution.

**First, tighten what “must do the work” means.** Your repository already contains the cautionary example: finding 10 reports that blocking the answer's access to the prompt makes zero thoughts score roughly 49–52%, but one thought already reaches 93–94%, close to six. I read these reported results; I did not recompute their raw runs. A zero-thought ablation can therefore establish an information bottleneck while missing a one-shot answer transported through that bottleneck. Require both early-budget controls and interventions on the *content* of interior states. [Repository finding 10](https://github.com/PeterLurf/latent-reasoning/blob/main/report/findings.md)

Nor does a failed direct-answer baseline establish, before training, that a task requires multiple GPT-2 calls. Twelve transformer layers do not mean twelve graph hops: global attention permits parallel composition. Relevant expressivity results depend on asymptotics and computational assumptions, not a numerical depth cutoff for GPT-2 small. Treat “our registered one-pass baseline fails” and “information cannot reach the answer before round d” as different claims. [Transformers, parallel computation, and logarithmic depth](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/sanford24a.html), [The expressive power of transformers with chain of thought](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07923)

**Design 1: six categorical thoughts, with exact answer-only credit assignment.**

Use S3 permutation composition, with each of the six operations drawn independently and uniformly. GPT-2 receives only a code embedding, the current operation symbol, and a fixed marker. A six-way head chooses the next code; discard logits, activations, and KV cache, then feed back only that code's embedding. Positions restart identically at every call. The initial code is fixed. A terminal call decodes the final permutation into one of six answer labels. The wrapper supplies operations in their given order but never multiplies permutations.

This gives two useful constraints. An omitted independent uniform operation makes the final product uniform conditional on every retained operation, so a stream processor without memory is at **1/6 chance**. And a six-code state has only six possible carried configurations, provided no other channel crosses the boundary. It cannot accumulate arbitrarily fine analog drift. Neither property guarantees correct transitions.

The main change from your failed hard-label experiments is **exact marginalization during training, not a straight-through estimator**. Evaluate GPT-2 on every code/operation pair to obtain learned transition probabilities T_a(i,j). For an example, propagate α_t(j)=Σ_i α_(t−1)(i)T_a_t(i,j), then minimize the negative log probability of its final answer under the learned decoder. Only 36 distinct transition inputs and six decoder inputs require GPT-2 evaluation per optimizer update; the same differentiable probabilities serve many sampled sequences. There are no intermediate target states. At inference, run actual GPT-2 calls and retain one argmax code after each operation: no probability-vector feedback, expected embedding, marginalization, or replacement by a transition-table executor.

Train on complete examples of lengths 1, 2, 4, and 8 under a fixed curriculum. Test at 8, 16, 32, 64, and 128. Uniform S3 products prevent frequency shortcuts; fresh online sequences prevent memorization. The six-way answer is preferable to parity, which needs only two states. If a binary benchmark is important, reveal a random three-element subset of S3 *only at the terminal query* and ask membership. Balanced yes/no answers across all 20 subsets still require distinguishing all six products; merely predicting parity cannot suffice.

In addition to the common controls, evaluate first-operation-only and last-operation-only execution, two-operation execution, and same-call-count resets. For six-way output, the null confidence bound is at most **18.67%**. Train the full-prompt baseline on the same operation strings. Its failure is required for the stronger empirical claim, because the streaming model's necessity otherwise follows partly from withholding future inputs. On an interior update, substitute a donor code and score the answer obtained by composing the donor's decoded state with the recipient's remaining suffix; this tests what information the latent actually carries.

The decisive audit is cheap: decode all six hard codes, require a bijection to S3, and check **all 36 hard transitions** against group composition after training. Together with correct initialization/readout, zero transition errors imply arbitrary-length correctness by induction for a stationary deterministic implementation. Verify stable argmax decisions and margins in the actual numerical mode; the certificate is conditional on those choices remaining unchanged. This is stronger than observing 100% “valid permutations.”

The nearest precedent is finite learnable recurrent states in State-Regularized RNNs, with differentiable stochastic finite-state machines providing a particularly close training idea. Your contribution would be the pretrained GPT-2 transition cell, exact terminal-likelihood training, hard neural execution, and complete causal audit—not the invention of finite-state recurrence. [State-Regularized RNNs](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/wang19j.html), [Differentiable finite-state machines](https://google-research.github.io/self-organising-systems/2022/diff-fsm/)

**Likeliest failure:** a collapsed or stochastic solution has good marginalized likelihood but bad greedy rollouts. Exact gradients remove one optimization problem; they do not remove local minima. Also, GPT-2 is an extravagant six-state transition parameterization: a tiny learned table or MLP may match it. Include that control. Success would establish compositional latent execution, not a special benefit from language pretraining or unbounded memory.

**Design 2: local hidden registers, with a provable communication boundary.**

Build Boolean expression trees with opaque tags for NAND, NOR, XOR, and XNOR. Leaves contain input bits; internal wire values are never supplied. Each internal node carries one of two nontextual latent embeddings. Use dependency-ready wavefronts: evaluate a node once, after both child states exist, batching independent nodes. The shared GPT-2 processes a short record containing its gate tag and its two children's **already completed** states. Keep completed inputs separate from newly proposed outputs. The wrapper supplies topology and dependency order; it does not evaluate gates. This scheduling is a substantial disclosed inductive bias, but costs O(nodes) neural calls rather than O(nodes × depth).

Every one of GPT-2's twelve layers stays inside the same local record. There is no current-wavefront neighbor attention, self-history, clock, global token, or retained KV. Consequently a leaf d edges from the root cannot influence it before d sequential wavefronts. Pair an example with one differing only in a remote leaf whose flip changes the correct answer. Before that information arrives, the two root states are identical and paired accuracy is exactly 50%. This is an actual necessity certificate for the specified local architecture. It follows from supplied communication constraints, so it cannot alone count as discovery of reasoning.

Again avoid straight-through learning. Enumerate the four hard child configurations for each gate to obtain a learned categorical kernel K_g(c|a,b). During training propagate q_v(c)=Σ_(a,b)q_left(a)q_right(b)K_g(c|a,b), then optimize only final-root cross-entropy. This is exact for disjoint-child trees with independent stochastic local updates. **It is not generally exact for reconvergent DAGs**, where child states can share stochastic ancestry. At test, use hard registers and real GPT-2 calls. Training needs only 16 distinct gate/child kernel evaluations per update; cheap marginal propagation supplies the gradient through many trees.

Use complete depth-1/2 examples first, then train through depth 8. Short final-answer curricula are allowed here, but be candid: they expose all local truth configurations through complete tasks. The final training band uses 255-node trees of depths 7–8; test depths 8, 16, 32, and 64 at the same node count using increasingly thin trees with varied side subtrees, plus held-out branching shapes. Randomize IDs/order and balance outputs within shape/operator strata. Generate sensitized remote-leaf pairs using a solver that remains inside the data generator; also test ordinary sampled trees so the benchmark is not exclusively a specially conditioned distribution.

Full execution takes d sequential wavefronts, although independent nodes run in parallel. Truncation after zero, one, or two wavefronts and the separately trained reset-register control must have upper confidence bounds ≤52% on deep paired tests; unreadied roots retain the same constant initial state. In the reset control, follow the identical schedule but replace computed child registers with the initial constant whenever they cross a node boundary. Full-prompt GPT-2 receives the same semantic input as a lossless prefix tree, using one token per gate/leaf to fit its context. Also give it structure-aware positions in a registered variant, so failure cannot simply reflect the local model receiving parsed wiring. Donor-register swaps at interior wires must match the solver's counterfactual output; swaps on irrelevant branches provide the negative control. Enumerate the learned gate/child transitions after training. Correct input/output encoding and zero errors across all local cases certify correct arbitrary-depth tree evaluation, conditional on stable hard choices. A completed wavefront schedule becoming idle is supplied stopping behavior, not learned halting or extra-iteration robustness.

This is distinct from your reachability marking channel: nonmonotone gate semantics prevent a generic “mark more” strategy, all local cases fit in a tiny audit, and exact likelihood replaces straight-through credit assignment. Nevertheless, supplied wiring is a substantial algorithmic scaffold. You would demonstrate that GPT-2 learns and executes local rules, not that it discovers a decomposition from raw prose.

The closest current prior is especially close: Ito and colleagues' 2026 preprint constructs a universal transformer for circuit evaluation and studies learning from depth-1/2 examples. GraphFSA likewise learns discrete local graph dynamics through a differentiable relaxation. Neither establishes this GPT-2 experiment's outcome. Your differences are the pretrained processor, single final-query supervision, exact tree marginalization, and stricter causal controls; novelty should be claimed narrowly. [Universal Transformers for Circuit Computations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31067), [GraphFSA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11042)

**Likeliest failure:** fractional training dynamics harden badly, or XOR credit assignment stalls before the shallow curriculum establishes the local rules. A second risk is that a tiny MLP learns equally well, exposing how much the supplied architecture contributes. That result would still be a useful positive control, but would narrow the scientific claim.

**Design 3: continuous local registers with self-trained repair.**

Keep the tree interface and dependency-ready schedule, but replace each categorical register with a bounded 16-dimensional vector. GPT-2 proposes the next vector from the gate and completed child vectors. The **same GPT-2 weights**, invoked with a fixed repair-mode embedding, then receive only that proposed vector and return a cleaned vector. Use one cleanup call initially. No second LM, codebook, text trace, neighboring input, or step index enters repair; include its extra call in every compute comparison. The causal communication boundary still forces recurrent state use, while continuous errors can accumulate along the tree's depth.

After depth-1/2 acquisition, train repair on noisy copies of the model's own states: minimize distance from the repaired perturbed vector to a stop-gradient copy of the original vector, alongside final-root answer loss. Add an idempotence objective only if preregistered. **This has final-answer-only external supervision, but final-answer cross-entropy is not its only loss.** No oracle intermediate states are used. If your rule forbids even self-generated hidden-state targets, preregister a separate noise-in-rollout variant trained only through final answers; do not quietly equate the two.

The hoped-for mechanism is several semantic basins with contraction *within* each basin. A globally contractive state map would erase distinctions that reasoning must retain. Bounding the norm only prevents divergence in magnitude; a repair fixed point need not be the correct state. This is therefore a hypothesis about error correction, **not an exact validity guarantee**.

Use the same tree generator, curriculum, depths, nulls, and ≥99% criterion as Design 2. Add two essential continuous controls: repair replaced by the identity, and identical repair calls trained only on final answers. At depth 64, require repair to improve accuracy by ≥10 points, or reduce error at least fivefold when both controls already exceed 90%. In a registered perturbation test, add independent Gaussian noise after every task update with per-coordinate RMS equal to 5% of the clean state's per-coordinate RMS, calibrated and frozen from the pilot. Require ≥95% depth-64 accuracy and at least fivefold lower error than the no-repair control. Semantic donor swaps should still cause the appropriate counterfactual answer: repair must not appear to erase meaningful substitutions through a hidden bypass.

State-Denoised RNNs are the nearest predecessor: they train attractor cleanup using corrupted versions of self-produced states and study final-target sequence tasks. Modern Hopfield networks clarify why convergence and correct retrieval are different properties. Reusing GPT-2 for both update and repair, with final-only external supervision and a strict causal audit, is the proposed adaptation; reliable GPT-2-scale acquisition remains unestablished. [State-Denoised Recurrent Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08394), [Hopfield Networks Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217)

**Likeliest failure:** repair stabilizes wrong or age-specific states, merges distinct semantic basins, or cannot undo a transition into the wrong basin. Actual continuous unrolling also costs much more than the first two designs' tiny shared transition tables during training. A throughput pilot that cannot fit useful training inside the cap should reject this implementation early.

**Your quantization candidate is worth keeping, with a narrower promise.**

Snapping guarantees membership in a codebook. It does not guarantee that codes represent valid task states, that the next code is correct, or that a wrong valid state can be repaired. Continuous drift can become discrete transition error: with conditional per-update error at most ε, a union bound gives sequence failure at most Tε. Thus ε≤0.01/128≈7.8×10⁻⁵ is a sufficient bound for 99% success at 128 updates; an exhaustive exact transition audit is more decisive. Start with snapping **after every transition**, then test intervals 2 and 4 only after that works. Reset every analog/KV/history/position bypass. A finite codebook can still encode a counter, especially when it has excess capacity.

Compare learned code geometry with fixed separated embeddings under the same categorical transition objective, and compare both with continuous states. VQ-VAE's standard recipe uses straight-through gradients and commitment/codebook losses; adopting it unchanged risks reproducing your failed hard-label optimization. Finite Scalar Quantization avoids learning codebook vectors, but its supplied rounding still uses straight-through gradients. Neither establishes trainability here. [VQ-VAE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00937), [Finite Scalar Quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15505)

I would prioritize **Design 1's six-state exact-gradient experiment**, then Design 2 if it passes. Compare six versus twelve codes only afterward to test whether spare capacity restores age dependence. The critical success is a correct hard transition system, not code utilization or visually separated clusters. Design 3 is a higher-risk diagnostic of continuous repair. If none learns its depth-1/2 cases reliably, stop before expensive extrapolation: the immediate bottleneck is rule acquisition. If the categorical rules certify but full-prompt baselines also succeed, you have solved stable latent execution under a controlled interface—and have accurately located what remains open in the stronger original goal.
