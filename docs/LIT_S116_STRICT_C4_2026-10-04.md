## The mechanism in note 112-2, and why it matters for the priors
- Past the cut (lengths 5–6 at k = 4), the final-answer target is predicted from the model's *own decoded checkpoint*
  after op 4, plus the remaining **1–2 ops**.
- So each such problem is, in effect, a **1–2-op short problem from a self-generated start state**. The cut
  manufactures short-problem footholds out of long final-answer problems.
- In leap-complexity terms (Abbe 2302.11055), the cut lowers the leap from L to L − k. That explains why acquisition
  appeared at L6 and not before, and why §85/§82's uncut length ≥ 3 learners sat on the symmetric saddle.
- A caveat that is also a mechanism: when the decoded checkpoint is wrong, the target (the true final state) is
  inconsistent with (decoded state + ops), which is label noise (our §62). Learning proceeds where the checkpoint is
  right, a self-reinforcing bootstrap.

## (a) Priors for outcome-only acquisition through a restart or cut
- **Bootstrapping from the learner's own states:** DAgger 1011.0686 (train on the states the learner visits);
  temporal-difference bootstrapping (Sutton 1988, non-arXiv) is the RL analogue of a target from your own next state.
- **Restarts from self-generated carry-over:**
  - Delethink 2510.06557 (RL with fixed-size chunks; the model learns to continue from its own carried text);
  - Markovian Transformers 2404.18988 (the answer must follow from the model's own CoT alone);
  - our Pilot L/M (restart-teacher distillation) and §65.
- **Length curricula and self-improvement:** Lee 2502.01612; 2511.07378 (recursive self-training provably extends
  length); Zhang 2607.20594 (joint training deadlocks, operator-first works).
- **Footholds:** §85 (≥ 3–10% short labels); §110b (random-start shorts, full-table footholds); Wies 2204.02892;
  Ruiz & Gu 2507.02782 (state coverage).
- **Not found:** acquisition of a non-solvable group's transitions from final answers only, through a history cut
  that turns long problems into self-started short ones. **New as scoped.** Cite DAgger, Delethink and Abbe as the
  conceptual anchors.

## (b) Is "op applied to the identity" (OPDEF) a fair "operator definition" qualifier?
- **Fair, with one note.** (e; a) labels each op's action on the identity: 120 problems, 0.83% of transitions, no
  composition. It is Zhang 2607.20594's operator-first curriculum in spirit.
- **It does leak something beyond vocabulary: the action convention.** The op token " perm p1..p5" already spells the
  permutation, but whether the arrangement after the op is p or p⁻¹ applied to positions is a convention that OPDEF
  resolves.
- Wording: "C4 qualified by operator definitions (each op on the identity; 120 problems, no composition)". NONE is the
  unqualified arm.

## (c) Design traps
1. **NONE's first rounds (L3, L4 ≤ k) have no cut**, so they are pure 3–4-op compositions from final answers: §85/§82's
   saddle. The mechanism only engages for L > k.
   - Start NONE's curriculum at **L = k + 1, k + 2** (5–6 at k = 4), or include those lengths from round 1.
   - Otherwise early rounds burn budget without footholds, and a NONE failure is uninterpretable.
2. **Bootstrap noise at the start.** At initialisation the decoded checkpoints are random arrangements, so post-cut
   targets are mostly inconsistent.
   - Log the fraction of post-cut problems whose decoded checkpoint is correct, per round. That is the "foothold
     yield" of the mechanism.
   - If it stays near chance (1/120), NONE stalls by construction.
3. **Naming.** With the gate keeping the pools empty, learning came from final-answer targets through cuts, not from
   pseudo-labels. Describe §116 as "final-answer training through self-decoded restarts", not as "filtered
   self-training".
4. **k matters:** effective footholds have length L mod k (or L − k). Report the per-length contribution. A k = 2
   variant would create 0–1-op tails, i.e. more footholds.
5. **C1 / C6 unchanged:** masked fork plus donor USE; ≥ 2/5 per the draft. Report all 5 seeds.
